🛹 The Skateboard Story

Chapter 1 of Spend Yourself Out of Debt (2020) — the first book of the S.A.F.E. program (Savings Actually Funds Everything). Free to listen or read, about 8 minutes.

An eight-year-old, a five-dollar allowance, and a skateboard he couldn't afford — and the savings account that taught his dad more than the dad taught him. This is the whole idea, in one story: borrow from your own savings, pay yourself back with interest, and watch the account grow because you spent from it.

🎧 Listen to Chapter 1

Read aloud for easy listening. The book's week-by-week charts are read in words — and shown below so you can see the numbers.

The first loan, week by week

Five weeks of saving one dollar a week. In week 6 he borrows all six dollars and buys the skateboard. Then two dollars a week go back in — the regular dollar, plus a dollar repaying the loan:

Week123456789101112
In the safe$1$2$3$4$5$0$2$4$6$8$10$12

Twelve weeks in, the safe holds $12 — exactly what it would have held if he had never borrowed. And he had the skateboard the whole time.

The second loan, in short

  • He borrows $13 for a better skateboard, and offers to repay over 14 weeks — the extra week is interest, paid to himself.
  • When it's done, the safe holds $1 more than if he had never borrowed.
  • He got the skateboard first and paid later — like a credit card, except the interest went into his own account.

Where this goes next

The second book, Spend Your Money Twice, takes the same idea deeper — into paying bills, reducing credit card debt, and building real financial security. It's the flagship of the S.A.F.E. program, which Gary runs today with participants in Indiana: each person builds an account in their own name that they can borrow from, paying the interest to themselves, until the fund can carry a real emergency.
